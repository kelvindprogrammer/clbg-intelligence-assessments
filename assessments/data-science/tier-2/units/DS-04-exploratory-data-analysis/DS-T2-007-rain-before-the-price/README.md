## B. Problem Statement

A food-security analysis unit (in a state ministry of agriculture, an NGO or a development partner) wants an early-warning rule of thumb: *when the rains fail in a state, which staple prices rise there, and how long afterwards?* If the answer is "millet and sorghum in the Sahel states, two to four months later", the unit can pre-position stocks or cash transfers before the lean season hits. If the answer is wrong, money goes to the wrong places at the wrong time.

You are given 12 years (2014–2025) of monthly data for 18 Nigerian states:

- **rainfall** per state (millimetres per month);
- **retail prices** of eight staples in each state-capital market (maize, millet, sorghum, local rice, cowpea, yam, garri and tomato), with gaps where the market did not report;
- **national macro series**: the official exchange rate, the pump price of petrol and a national food price index.

Nigerian food prices over this period were dominated by **national shocks** that have nothing to do with local rain: the 2016 currency crisis, the 2019 land-border closure and the 2020 pandemic, and in mid-2023 the removal of the petrol subsidy and the unification of exchange rates. Every price in every state rose together. Rainfall, meanwhile, is strongly seasonal, varies across agro-ecological zones and has its own trends. Correlate raw prices with raw rainfall and you will "discover" dozens of links that are nothing but shared trends and coincident timing. Try seven lags, two rainfall definitions and 144 state-commodity pairs, and you will find "significant" results by chance alone.

**Your job is an exploratory investigation that holds up**: explore the panel, decide how to isolate a local rainfall signal, choose and justify your method, compare it with a deliberately naive baseline, control for the number of hypotheses you examined, and deliver a **hypothesis register**: one row per hypothesis, each marked *supported*, *exploratory*, *not supported* or *insufficient data*, with an effect size, an interval and a follow-up action.

**What makes it hard.**

- The confounders are large and national, the signal is small and local, and it appears with a delay.
- Lags, features and pairs create a garden of forking paths. Choosing the best-looking lag and then reporting its p-value as if it were the only one you tried is the classic error.
- Monthly series are autocorrelated and seasonal, so textbook p-values from ordinary regression are too small.
- Price gaps are not random everywhere. In some conflict-affected states, markets stop reporting precisely when conditions are worst.

**Known facts** (your only documentation):

- Rainfall is a single monthly figure per state. Prices are from one market per state, the state capital.
- Real state names and approximate capital coordinates are used for realism, but **every number is synthetic**, generated by CLBG to resemble the structure of real Nigerian data. Do not cite any figure from this dataset as a fact about Nigeria.
- The assessor knows which rainfall–price links are real in this synthetic world. You do not.

## C. Objectives

When you finish, you should be able to show that you can:

1. **Align and explore a multi-source panel**, documenting coverage, missingness patterns, seasonality and trends before modelling.
2. **Isolate a local signal from common shocks** and justify the method (for example deflation, cross-sectional differencing, or fixed effects).
3. **Explore lags and feature definitions without self-deception**, using inference that accounts for the search.
4. **Control the false discovery rate** across many hypotheses and report what that guarantees and what it does not.
5. **Compare your method with a naive baseline** quantitatively, on data where the truth is known (your own simulation), and on the real panel.
6. **Produce a hypothesis register** that separates confirmed-for-now from exploratory findings and assigns each a concrete follow-up.

### D.1 Mandatory requirements

The scaffolding is deliberately light. [`starter/rainprice/`](starter/rainprice/) gives you the data loader (`load_panel`), the register contract (`REGISTER_COLUMNS`, `STATUSES`, `validate_register`), a scorer (`score_register`) and the naive baseline (`naive_register`). **The method is yours.**

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **R1** | `build_register(panel) -> DataFrame` in `rainprice/register.py`. It returns at least one row for **every** (state, commodity) pair, with exactly the columns `hypothesis_id, state, commodity, rain_feature, lag_months, effect, ci_low, ci_high, p_value, q_value, status, method, follow_up` and `status` in `supported / exploratory / not_supported / insufficient_data`. It must pass `validate_register`, be deterministic, and run in under 5 minutes. Supported rows need an effect with an interval and a **multiplicity-adjusted** `q_value`. | **R5** | **Sensitivity.** Show how your claimed set changes under at least two reasonable alternative choices (for example deflating by the food CPI instead of your approach, a different anomaly window, or a different FDR level). Name which claims are robust. |

## G. Acceptance Criteria

⚙ = automated (hidden suite on the official panel and two re-generated panels with **different** true links); ✍ = assessor.

1. ⚙ `build_register` satisfies the contract, is deterministic and runs in under 5 minutes on each hidden panel.
2. ⚙ **Recall ≥ 0.60 and FDR ≤ 0.30** of claimed (supported) pairs on every hidden panel. *Justification:* the planted effects are about 0.5–1 standard deviation of the residual price noise and persist for 12 years, so a method that removes common shocks and seasonality detects most of them (the reference recovers 0.82–0.94 with FDR 0–0.22); an FDR above 0.30 means that roughly one in three "supported" links would send resources to the wrong place, which a food-security team should not accept.
3. ⚙ **Lag accuracy ≥ 0.70** (claimed true links whose reported lag is within one month of the true lag). *Justification:* a rule of thumb with the wrong timing is useless for pre-positioning; ±1 month allows for the smoothing of anomaly windows.
4. ⚙ **Beats the naive baseline** by at least 0.30 in recall **and** at least 0.30 in FDR on every hidden panel. *Justification:* the baseline's FDR is about 0.85–0.95 and its recall about 0.05–0.20 on these panels, so a method that genuinely handles trends, seasonality and multiplicity clears this margin easily, while cosmetic changes to the baseline do not.
5. ⚙ At most 3 claimed links on the four macro-driven commodities (rice, garri, yam, cowpea) that are not true links. *Justification:* this checks specifically that national shocks were removed; BH at q = 0.10 expects fewer than two false discoveries in total.
6. ✍ The exploration (R2) precedes and justifies the method; the simulation study (R3) and sensitivity analysis (R5) are present and honestly reported, including failures.
7. ✍ The register's *exploratory* rows have specific, feasible follow-ups, and the report explains what "supported" does and does not mean (an association in this panel, not a causal effect, and not a forecast).
8. ✍ Informative missingness is detected and its consequences discussed.

## Pedagogical Design

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge needed** | Regression, seasonality and trend, p-values, multiple testing, pandas reshaping. |
| **New skill developed** | Running a many-hypothesis exploration that controls its own error rate, validated by simulation against a baseline. |
| **Decisions the member must make** | How to remove national shocks; how to define a rainfall anomaly; which lags and features to search; how to get valid p-values under autocorrelation and search; which FDR level; what counts as exploratory. |
| **What must be investigated independently** | Missingness patterns, the role of the macro series, the 2022 coincidence, the right null distribution, and how the method behaves on simulated data. |
| **Why it's interesting** | A real policy question in which the naive answer is confidently wrong in both directions: it misses the real links and invents false ones. |
| **Evidence of competence** | Hidden-truth recall, FDR and lag accuracy on re-generated panels with different true links; a simulation study; a defended register. |
| **Misconceptions exposed** | "Correlate the raw series." "Pick the best lag and report its p-value." "p < 0.05 across 144 pairs means 144 tests at 5%." "Missing data is random." "A supported link is a causal effect." |
| **Preparation for later work** | DS-08 (causal inference with panels), DS-10 (time series), DS-T3-005 (confirmatory versus exploratory findings). |

**Suggested approach** (optional): plot four or five raw series before computing anything; look at the cross-state average price; build the simulator early, because it is the only place where you can see whether your method works.
