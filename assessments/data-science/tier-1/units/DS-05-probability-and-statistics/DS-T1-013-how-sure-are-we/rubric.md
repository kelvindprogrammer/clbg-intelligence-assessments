# DS-T1-013 Evaluation Rubric (public)

This rubric is public so you know exactly how you'll be judged. Assessors also use a confidential marking guide with calibration examples, but it never adds criteria that aren't listed here.

## Weighted dimensions

| # | Dimension | Points | What the assessor looks for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem understanding and technical reasoning | 10 | You explain what "95% confidence" promises, why skewness and rare events break that promise, and why the side of the miss matters to the manager. |
| 2 | Technical correctness | 20 | Closed-form intervals match `scipy` exactly; the bootstrap and BCa endpoints agree within Monte Carlo error; the Wilson derivation is correct; edge cases (k = 0, constant samples) behave as specified. |
| 3 | Implementation quality | 10 | A vectorised bootstrap with no loop over B; all randomness through `rng`; methods as interchangeable callables; no duplicated logic between the interval types. |
| 4 | Methodology: experiment design and comparison | 20 | The full grid run reproducibly; Monte Carlo SE reported and used when comparing; miss sides analysed; the recommendation follows from the evidence rather than from textbook habit. |
| 5 | Testing and evaluation | 15 | At least 10 member tests, including hand-computed jackknife and Wilson cases and a test that catches resampling without replacement. |
| 6 | Documentation and reproducibility | 10 | The CLI regenerates both CSVs exactly; figures regenerate from a script; seeds stated. |
| 7 | Critical thinking and limitations | 10 | Says how far the study population's conclusions transfer to each real LGA; notes the simple-random-sampling assumption; doesn't over-claim small coverage differences. |
| 8 | Presentation and communication | 5 | A manager's table and paragraph that a non-statistician can act on, with no zero-width intervals and an honest note about skewed means. |
| | **Total** | **100** | |

## Performance levels

| Level | Share of points | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| **Exemplary** | 90–100% | Correct and efficient, with insight: for example, explaining Wald's oscillating coverage, or showing why BCa helps the median more than the mean. |
| **Proficient** | 70–89% | All requirements met; the comparison is sound, with minor gaps in error-bar use or explanation. |
| **Developing** | 50–69% | Methods mostly work, but the comparison ignores Monte Carlo error or miss sides, or the recommendation does not follow from the numbers. |
| **Beginning** | 0–49% | Bootstrap or BCa incorrect, the coverage study missing or not reproducible, or zero-width intervals reported to the manager. |

## Gates (pass/fail, independent of points)

1. **Integrity:** `AI_USE_AND_SOURCES.md` is present and consistent with what the defence shows.
2. **Reproducibility:** the assessor reruns the CLI from a clean environment and gets the submitted CSVs.
3. **Defence:** the member computes a Wilson interval for k = 0 and handles the unseen modification with sound reasoning.

## Automatic deductions

| Issue | Deduction |
|---|---|
| The survey file was modified (checksum mismatch) | Gate fail until corrected |
| `scipy.stats.bootstrap` used inside the package | Up to −10 from dimension 2 |
| Coverage reported without Monte Carlo error anywhere | Up to −5 from dimension 4 |
| Report numbers not regenerated by the code | Up to −5 from dimension 6 |
